\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider the manuscript ``Where Laboratory Automation Work Breaks: A Mixed-Methods Study of Public Practitioner Discussions'' as Original Research.

The paper analyzes 55 purposively selected public laboratory-automation discussions and 45 analytical episodes. It contributes a layered bottleneck taxonomy, bounded metrics with explicit denominators, relationship analysis, and community artifacts for reproducible troubleshooting and knowledge retention. The manuscript's central technical result connects execution observability with recovery after irreversible physical actions. Its methodological contribution shows how public practitioner evidence can support operational measurement design without converting complaint counts into prevalence or vendor-reliability claims.

The work is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. The associated repository contains the LaTeX source, derived de-identified data, executable analyses, schemas, tests, and a reproducible paper build. The public release does not redistribute a verbatim forum corpus or contributor handles.

I believe the manuscript fits SLAS Technology because it addresses device integration, method deployment, physical-resource models, scheduling, observability, recovery, validation, and AI-assisted method development through evidence drawn from the laboratory-automation practitioner community.

\closing{Sincerely,}
