\begin{letter}{Guest Editors and Editors\\NexusXp: The Connected Lab\\SLAS Technology}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider ``Where Laboratory Automation Work Breaks: A Mixed-Methods Study of Public Practitioner Discussions'' as Original Research for the SLAS Technology special issue ``NexusXp: The Connected Lab.'' I would also welcome placement in the journal's regular collection if the editors consider that route more appropriate.

The manuscript analyzes 55 purposively selected public laboratory-automation discussions and 45 analytical episodes. It contributes a layered bottleneck taxonomy, bounded metrics with explicit denominators, relationship analysis, and implementable community artifacts for reproducible troubleshooting and knowledge retention. Its central technical result connects execution observability with recovery after irreversible physical actions. Its methodological contribution shows how public practitioner evidence can support operational measurement design without converting complaint counts into prevalence or vendor-reliability claims.

The work directly addresses connected-laboratory infrastructure through device integration, method deployment, physical-resource models, scheduling, observability, recovery, validation, and AI-assisted method development. These topics align with the special issue's focus on advanced automation, data handling, connected workflows, and real-world integration challenges.

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published previously. The public repository contains the LaTeX source, derived de-identified data, executable analyses, schemas, tests, and reproducible build instructions. It excludes a verbatim forum corpus and contributor handles. A preprint and versioned archival package will be deposited with persistent identifiers before submission.

Thank you for considering this manuscript.

\closing{Sincerely,}
