\begin{letter}{Guest Editors and Editors\\NexusXp: The Connected Lab\\SLAS Technology}
\opening{Dear Editors,}

Please consider ``Where Connected Laboratory Automation Breaks: Operational Boundaries Revealed by Public Practitioner Evidence'' as Original Research for the SLAS Technology special issue ``NexusXp: The Connected Lab.'' Regular SLAS Technology would also be an appropriate transfer destination if the editors prefer that placement.

The manuscript addresses a practical gap in the connected-laboratory literature. Standards, orchestration systems, and autonomous-laboratory demonstrations describe what integrated systems can do; public practitioner evidence reveals where integration, deployment identity, physical representations, observability, recovery, and validation remain operationally under-specified. We analyze 55 purposively selected public discussions and 45 episodes from a deliberately difficult subset, then operationalize the resulting mechanisms over explicit case units.

The principal finding is boundary-centered. Connected-laboratory failures concentrate where knowledge must become a maintained artifact, a nominal interface must become testable device access, source must become an identified deployment, resource data must become a validated physical model, and execution evidence must support recovery after irreversible actions. Observability and recovery form the strongest descriptive relationship, and the paper provides leave-one-thread-out, score-weighting, and denominator sensitivity analyses. The work also releases executable metrics, a troubleshooting-question schema, a governed resolved-knowledge format, and designs for interface and event registries.

The paper fits NexusXp through its direct treatment of advanced automation integration, laboratory data handling, AI-assisted workflows, connected device operation, and real-world implementation constraints. It does not infer industry prevalence or vendor reliability from forum recurrence. The complete source, derived data, schemas, tests, and reproducible build are available in the versioned public release supplied with the submission.

The work is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been published as an archival article. Thank you for considering it.

\closing{Sincerely,}
