\title{Maintaining the Connected Laboratory: Articulation, Repair, and Knowledge Stewardship in a Public Technical Community}

\begin{abstract}
Laboratory automation is maintained through collaborative work that crosses software, instruments, physical materials, vendor boundaries, and scientific practice. We study how this work becomes visible in a public specialist forum. The analysis covers 55 purposively selected discussions and 45 episodes from a deliberately difficult 14-thread subset. We identify ten recurrent bottlenecks and examine how participants articulate hidden dependencies, reconstruct partially executed physical processes, move between public and private support channels, and transform local diagnosis into reusable infrastructure. Three findings organize the paper. First, practitioners perform articulation work by eliciting the versions, interfaces, physical constraints, and validation conditions omitted from initial problem formulations. Second, repair requires reconciliation of software histories with irreversible physical actions and uncertain material state. Third, public knowledge remains durable only when community members convert discussion into maintained code, post-mortems, schemas, or indexed records with ownership and correction pathways. We contribute a boundary-centered account of connected-laboratory maintenance and derive design implications for public technical communities, support systems, observability infrastructure, and community-maintained knowledge records. The study is a purposive single-coder pilot and does not estimate field-wide incidence or product reliability.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{laboratory automation, articulation work, repair, infrastructure, technical communities, knowledge stewardship, CSCW}
