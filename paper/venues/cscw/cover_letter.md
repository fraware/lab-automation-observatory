## Scope statement

The paper makes a CSCW contribution by examining the cooperative work through which practitioners maintain a connected cyber-physical environment. Using 55 purposively selected public laboratory-automation discussions and 45 analytical episodes, we show how participants articulate hidden dependencies, coordinate repair across software and irreversible physical action, negotiate validation evidence, move between public and private support channels, and convert local diagnosis into maintained infrastructure. The contribution is organized around articulation work, infrastructural breakdown, repair, and knowledge stewardship. The paper derives design implications for progressive troubleshooting interfaces, linked run-event records, public return paths after private support, evidence-graded community records, and evaluation of technical communities through knowledge outcomes.

The paper does not present discussion recurrence as field prevalence, product reliability, or causal evidence. The empirical register is a purposive single-coder pilot, and the manuscript reports no agreement statistic.
